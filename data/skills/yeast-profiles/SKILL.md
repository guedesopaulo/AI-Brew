# Yeast Profiles Reference

Format: Strain | Attenuation | Temp Range | Flavor Notes | Best Styles | Flocculation | Alcohol Tolerance | Pressure Fermenting | Co-fermentation

## American Ale Yeasts

### Wyeast 1056 / WLP001 / SafAle US-05 (American Ale)
Attenuation: 75–78% | Temp: 16–22°C (US-05 up to 24°C)
Flavor: Clean, neutral, versatile — lets hops and malt shine
Best for: American IPA, West Coast IPA, APA, American Amber, American Stout, DIPA
Flocculation: Medium-Low
Alcohol tolerance: up to 11% ABV
Pressure fermenting: Yes — 10–15 psi at 18–22°C produces very clean, ester-suppressed ale; popular "lager-style" clean ale technique
Co-fermentation: Blends well with 1272 (50/50) for slightly more complexity while retaining cleanliness
Notes: The gold standard workhorse. Very forgiving. High pitch rate for clean results. US-05 (dry): cheaper, same profile, slightly lower attenuation at lower temps.

### Wyeast 1272 / WLP051 (American Ale II)
Attenuation: 72% | Temp: 16–22°C
Flavor: Slightly fruity, nutty, clean
Best for: American Amber Ale, APA, Brown Ale
Flocculation: Medium-High
Alcohol tolerance: up to 10% ABV
Pressure fermenting: Yes — 10 psi; subtle nut/fruit remains even under pressure
Co-fermentation: Classic 1056 + 1272 blend (50/50) for cleaner American ales with slight nutty complexity
Notes: Slight nut/fruit adds complexity without overpowering. Less clean than 1056.

### Wyeast 1332 (Northwest Ale)
Attenuation: 71% | Temp: 16–22°C
Flavor: Malty, clean, slightly fruity
Best for: American Amber, Brown Ale, cream ale variants
Flocculation: High
Alcohol tolerance: up to 10% ABV
Pressure fermenting: Yes
Co-fermentation: Can blend with 1056 for more attenuation in malt-forward styles
Notes: Malt-forward profile. Leaves slightly higher FG than 1056.

## English / British Ale Yeasts

### Wyeast 1968 / WLP002 (London Ale / English Ale)
Attenuation: 67–70% | Temp: 16–22°C
Flavor: Biscuity, fruity esters, slightly sweet finish
Best for: ESB, English Bitter, English Porter, Oatmeal Stout, Mild
Flocculation: Very High (may need rousing mid-fermentation)
Alcohol tolerance: up to 10% ABV
Pressure fermenting: Not recommended — already low attenuation; pressure reduces it further
Co-fermentation: Sometimes blended with 1056 for ESB variants needing more attenuation
Notes: Flocculates aggressively — rouse or raise temp before crash. Higher FG adds body. Classic British pub character.

### SafAle S-04 (Dry English Ale)
Attenuation: 70% | Temp: 15–22°C
Flavor: Fruity, biscuity, slightly sweet
Best for: ESB, English Bitter, Porter
Flocculation: Very High
Alcohol tolerance: up to 10% ABV
Pressure fermenting: Yes — low pressure (5–8 psi) reduces esters for cleaner English profile
Co-fermentation: N/A
Notes: Dry version of English ale character. Very high flocculation — crystal-clear beer.

### Wyeast 1084 / WLP004 (Irish Ale)
Attenuation: 71–73% | Temp: 16–22°C
Flavor: Slightly fruity, dry finish with roasty undertone
Best for: Irish Dry Stout, Irish Red, ESB
Flocculation: Medium
Alcohol tolerance: up to 12% ABV
Pressure fermenting: Moderate — 10 psi acceptable
Co-fermentation: N/A
Notes: Dry finish enhances roasty character in stouts. Subtle fruitiness.

### Wyeast 1098 / WLP007 (British Ale)
Attenuation: 73% | Temp: 16–22°C
Flavor: Fruity, clean, crisp
Best for: English Bitter, ESB, English IPA, Pale Ale
Flocculation: Medium-High
Alcohol tolerance: up to 12% ABV
Pressure fermenting: Yes
Co-fermentation: Good partner for English-style blends
Notes: More attenuative than 1968. Clean with slight fruitiness. Versatile English strain.

### Wyeast 1450 (Denny's Favourite 50)
Attenuation: 74% | Temp: 16–22°C
Flavor: Full-bodied, malty, clean, rich
Best for: American Amber, Brown Ale, Barleywine, bigger English ales
Flocculation: Low-Medium
Alcohol tolerance: up to 12% ABV
Pressure fermenting: Not recommended — mouthfeel character depends on unfermented sugars; pressure may reduce them further
Co-fermentation: N/A
Notes: Exceptional body and mouthfeel. Retains malt sweetness better than 1056.

## German / Central European Yeasts

### Wyeast 3068 (Weihenstephan Weizen)
Attenuation: 73% | Temp: 17–24°C
Flavor: Banana (isoamyl acetate) + clove (4-vinyl guaiacol)
Best for: Hefeweizen, Dunkelweizen, Weizenbock
Flocculation: Low (powdery — stays in suspension; beer naturally hazy)
Alcohol tolerance: up to 10% ABV
Pressure fermenting: No — pressure suppresses isoamyl acetate and 4-VG that define the style
Co-fermentation: Sometimes used with Conan for hybrid wheat/IPA applications
Notes: **Temperature drives character** — ferment 17°C for clove, 21°C+ for banana. Most authentic German wheat yeast. Soft water preferred.

### Wyeast 2565 (Kölsch)
Attenuation: 73% | Temp: 13–18°C
Flavor: Clean, slightly fruity, soft
Best for: Kölsch, light German ales
Flocculation: Medium-Low
Alcohol tolerance: up to 10% ABV
Pressure fermenting: Yes — 5–10 psi at 14–16°C accelerates lager-like character; pair with cold conditioning
Co-fermentation: N/A
Notes: Ale yeast fermented cold for lager-like character. Lager condition for 2 weeks at 2°C.

### Wyeast 2112 (California Lager)
Attenuation: 70% | Temp: 14–18°C
Flavor: Clean, malty, smooth with slight fruitiness at upper range
Best for: California Common (Steam Beer), cream ales at warm fermentation
Flocculation: High
Alcohol tolerance: up to 10% ABV
Pressure fermenting: Yes — unique trait: 12–15 psi at 18°C produces clean lager character; this is the classic California Common technique
Co-fermentation: N/A
Notes: Ferments warmer than true lager yeasts. Unique hybrid character.

### Wyeast 2124 / WLP830 (Bohemian Lager)
Attenuation: 71% | Temp: 8–14°C
Flavor: Clean, malty, crisp — slight maltiness
Best for: Bohemian Pilsner, German Pilsner, Helles, Märzen
Flocculation: Medium
Alcohol tolerance: up to 11% ABV
Pressure fermenting: Yes — 12–20 psi at 18–20°C; suppresses esters for clean lager at ale temps. Popular for fast lager production.
Co-fermentation: Blends with 2308 for added Märzen complexity
Notes: Needs long cold fermentation (10–12°C) and lagering (4–6 weeks at 2°C) for traditional results.

### Wyeast 2206 (Bavarian Lager)
Attenuation: 73% | Temp: 8–14°C
Flavor: Clean, malty, rich
Best for: Bock, Doppelbock, Märzen, Dunkel
Flocculation: Medium
Alcohol tolerance: up to 12% ABV
Pressure fermenting: Yes — 12–15 psi at 18°C works well
Co-fermentation: N/A
Notes: Richer, more malt-forward than 2124. Classic Bavarian lager character.

### SafLager W-34/70 (Dry Lager)
Attenuation: 80% | Temp: 9–15°C
Flavor: Clean, crisp, lager-character
Best for: Any lager style — extremely versatile
Flocculation: High
Alcohol tolerance: up to 14% ABV
Pressure fermenting: Yes — **the most popular pressure lager strain**: 12–20 psi at 18–22°C produces clean lager at ale fermentation temps in 5–7 days. Standard commercial practice.
Co-fermentation: Sometimes blended 50/50 with S-189 for added complexity in Märzen/Pilsner
Notes: Most-used commercial lager yeast worldwide. High attenuation produces dry finish. Excellent flocculation.

### Wyeast 2308 (Munich Lager)
Attenuation: 72% | Temp: 8–14°C
Flavor: Smooth, clean, malty
Best for: Bock, Munich Helles, Märzen
Flocculation: Medium
Alcohol tolerance: up to 11% ABV
Pressure fermenting: Yes
Co-fermentation: Blends with 2124 for standard lager hybrids
Notes: Similar to 2206 but smoother profile.

## Belgian Ale Yeasts

### Wyeast 1388 (Belgian Strong Ale)
Attenuation: 78% | Temp: 18–26°C
Flavor: Fruity, spicy, warming, complex esters
Best for: Belgian Golden Strong Ale, Tripel, Strong Dark
Flocculation: Low
Alcohol tolerance: up to 12% ABV
Pressure fermenting: No — ester and phenol development essential to Belgian character
Co-fermentation: N/A
Notes: High attenuation dries out big beers. Fruity at lower temps, drier/spicier at higher.

### Wyeast 1762 (Belgian Abbey II)
Attenuation: 74% | Temp: 18–26°C
Flavor: Raisin, prune, spicy, fruity
Best for: Belgian Dubbel, Quadrupel, Strong Dark Ale
Flocculation: Medium-Low
Alcohol tolerance: up to 15% ABV
Pressure fermenting: No
Co-fermentation: N/A
Notes: Rich dark fruit character. Pairs perfectly with Special B and dark candi sugar.

### Wyeast 3787 (Trappist High Gravity)
Attenuation: 76% | Temp: 18–26°C
Flavor: Fruity, spicy, complex, warming
Best for: Belgian Tripel, Dubbel, Belgian Strong, Saison (secondary)
Flocculation: Medium
Alcohol tolerance: up to 14% ABV
Pressure fermenting: No
Co-fermentation: N/A
Notes: Handles high gravity well. Classic Trappist character. Ferment warm for complexity.

### Wyeast 3944 (Belgian Witbier)
Attenuation: 72% | Temp: 17–24°C
Flavor: Tart, fruity, spicy, phenolic
Best for: Witbier, Belgian White, Bière Blanche
Flocculation: Low
Alcohol tolerance: up to 10% ABV
Pressure fermenting: No — phenolic character (4-VG) must develop freely
Co-fermentation: N/A
Notes: Produces classic Witbier tartness and spice. Lower temps for more phenolic character.

### Wyeast 1318 / WLP066 (London Ale III)
Attenuation: 71–73% | Temp: 17–23°C
Flavor: Fruity, full-bodied, juicy
Best for: NEIPA / Hazy IPA (top choice), Hazy Pale, English Pale
Flocculation: High
Alcohol tolerance: up to 10% ABV
Pressure fermenting: No — NEIPA biotransformation requires normal warm fermentation; pressure would impede thiol conversion
Co-fermentation: Sometimes blended with 1272 for NEIPA variants with slightly less body
Notes: **Critical for NEIPA** — produces biotransformation compounds that enhance hop fruitiness. Retains residual sweetness and full body. Do NOT use for West Coast IPA (too fruity, not dry).

## Specialty Yeasts

### Kveik (Lallemand Voss)
Attenuation: 80% | Temp: 25–40°C
Flavor: Orange, tropical at lower temps (25–30°C); neutral at high temp (38–40°C)
Best for: Any style wanting fast fermentation (24–48h), farmhouse ales
Flocculation: High
Alcohol tolerance: up to 14% ABV
Pressure fermenting: Yes — works well at high temps + 10–15 psi; neutral clean profile at 38°C under pressure
Co-fermentation: N/A
Notes: Extreme heat tolerance. Fermenting at 25–30°C produces orange/citrus esters. At 38–40°C produces neutral, clean beer very quickly. No off-flavors at high temps. Can underpitch aggressively (0.1 M cells/mL/°P) — high temp and fast fermentation compensates.

## Attenuation & Body Notes

- **High attenuation (>75%)**: Dry finish, lower FG — use for IPA, Saison, Belgian Strong, any "dry" style
- **Low attenuation (<70%)**: Full body, residual sweetness — use for ESB, NEIPA, English stouts, malt-forward styles
- **FG target** = OG × (1 - attenuation %) — e.g., OG 1.060, 75% att → FG 1.015
- **Temperature effects**: Higher fermentation temp = more esters + fusel alcohols. Critical for Hefeweizen and Belgian styles.

## Pressure Fermenting Summary

Pressure fermenting uses a spunding valve (8–20 psi) to suppress ester and fusel alcohol production, enabling ale or lager yeast to ferment at higher-than-usual temperatures while producing clean, lager-like character.

| Yeast | Pressure | Temp | Result |
|-------|----------|------|--------|
| W-34/70 | 12–20 psi | 18–22°C | Clean lager in 5–7 days at ale temps |
| US-05 / 1056 | 10–15 psi | 18–22°C | Very clean, ester-suppressed American ale |
| WLP001 | 10–15 psi | 18–22°C | Same as US-05 |
| 2124 / WLP830 | 12–20 psi | 18–20°C | Lager character without cold fermentation |
| California Lager 2112 | 12–15 psi | 18°C | Classic California Common |

**Not suitable for pressure:** 3068 (Hefeweizen), Belgian strains (1388, 1762, 3787, 3944), London Ale III (1318/WLP066 — suppresses biotransformation).
