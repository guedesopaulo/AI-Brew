# Yeast Profiles Reference

Format: Strain | Attenuation | Temp Range | Flavor Notes | Best Styles

## American Ale Yeasts

### Wyeast 1056 / WLP001 / SafAle US-05 (American Ale)
Attenuation: 75–78% | Temp: 16–22°C (US-05 up to 24°C)
Flavor: Clean, neutral, versatile — lets hops and malt shine
Best for: American IPA, West Coast IPA, APA, American Amber, American Stout, DIPA
Notes: The gold standard workhorse. Very forgiving. High pitch rate for clean results.
US-05 (dry): Cheaper, same profile, slightly lower attenuation at lower temps.

### Wyeast 1272 / WLP051 (American Ale II)
Attenuation: 72% | Temp: 16–22°C
Flavor: Slightly fruity, nutty, clean
Best for: American Amber Ale, APA, Brown Ale
Notes: Slight nut/fruit adds complexity without overpowering. Less clean than 1056.

### Wyeast 1332 (Northwest Ale)
Attenuation: 71% | Temp: 16–22°C
Flavor: Malty, clean, slightly fruity
Best for: American Amber, Brown Ale, cream ale variants
Notes: Malt-forward profile. Leaves slightly higher FG than 1056.

## English / British Ale Yeasts

### Wyeast 1968 / WLP002 (London Ale / English Ale)
Attenuation: 67–70% | Temp: 16–22°C
Flavor: Biscuity, fruity esters, slightly sweet finish
Best for: ESB, English Bitter, English Porter, Oatmeal Stout, Mild
Notes: Flocculates aggressively — may need rousing. Higher FG adds body. Classic British pub character.

### SafAle S-04 (Dry English Ale)
Attenuation: 70% | Temp: 15–22°C
Flavor: Fruity, biscuity, slightly sweet
Best for: ESB, English Bitter, Porter
Notes: Dry version of English ale character. Very high flocculation.

### Wyeast 1084 / WLP004 (Irish Ale)
Attenuation: 71–73% | Temp: 16–22°C
Flavor: Slightly fruity, dry finish with roasty undertone
Best for: Irish Dry Stout, Irish Red, ESB
Notes: Dry finish enhances roasty character in stouts. Subtle fruitiness.

### Wyeast 1098 / WLP007 (British Ale)
Attenuation: 73% | Temp: 16–22°C
Flavor: Fruity, clean, crisp
Best for: English Bitter, ESB, English IPA, Pale Ale
Notes: More attenuative than 1968. Clean with slight fruitiness. Versatile English strain.

### Wyeast 1450 (Denny's Favourite 50)
Attenuation: 74% | Temp: 16–22°C
Flavor: Full-bodied, malty, clean, rich
Best for: American Amber, Brown Ale, Barleywine, bigger English ales
Notes: Exceptional body and mouthfeel. Retains malt sweetness better than 1056.

## German / Central European Yeasts

### Wyeast 3068 (Weihenstephan Weizen)
Attenuation: 73% | Temp: 17–24°C
Flavor: Banana (isoamyl acetate) + clove (4-vinyl guaiacol)
Best for: Hefeweizen, Dunkelweizen, Weizenbock
Notes: **Temperature drives character** — ferment 17°C for clove, 21°C+ for banana.
Most authentic German wheat yeast. Soft water preferred.

### Wyeast 2565 (Kölsch)
Attenuation: 73% | Temp: 13–18°C
Flavor: Clean, slightly fruity, soft
Best for: Kölsch, light German ales
Notes: Ale yeast fermented cold for lager-like character. Lager condition for 2 weeks at 2°C.

### Wyeast 2112 (California Lager)
Attenuation: 70% | Temp: 14–18°C
Flavor: Clean, malty, smooth with slight fruitiness at upper range
Best for: California Common (Steam Beer), cream ales at warm fermentation
Notes: Ferments warmer than true lager yeasts. Unique hybrid character.

### Wyeast 2124 / WLP830 (Bohemian Lager)
Attenuation: 71% | Temp: 8–14°C
Flavor: Clean, malty, crisp — slight maltiness
Best for: Bohemian Pilsner, German Pilsner, Helles, Märzen
Notes: Needs long cold fermentation (10–12°C) and lagering (4–6 weeks at 2°C).

### Wyeast 2206 (Bavarian Lager)
Attenuation: 73% | Temp: 8–14°C
Flavor: Clean, malty, rich
Best for: Bock, Doppelbock, Märzen, Dunkel
Notes: Richer, more malt-forward than 2124. Classic Bavarian lager character.

### SafLager W-34/70 (Dry Lager)
Attenuation: 80% | Temp: 9–15°C
Flavor: Clean, crisp, lager-character
Best for: Any lager style — extremely versatile
Notes: The most-used commercial lager yeast. High attenuation produces dry finish. Excellent flocculation.

### Wyeast 2308 (Munich Lager)
Attenuation: 72% | Temp: 8–14°C
Flavor: Smooth, clean, malty
Best for: Bock, Munich Helles, Märzen
Notes: Similar to 2206 but smoother profile.

## Belgian Ale Yeasts

### Wyeast 1388 (Belgian Strong Ale)
Attenuation: 78% | Temp: 18–26°C
Flavor: Fruity, spicy, warming, complex esters
Best for: Belgian Golden Strong Ale, Tripel, Strong Dark
Notes: High attenuation dries out big beers. Fruity at lower temps, drier/spicier at higher.

### Wyeast 1762 (Belgian Abbey II)
Attenuation: 74% | Temp: 18–26°C
Flavor: Raisin, prune, spicy, fruity
Best for: Belgian Dubbel, Quadrupel, Strong Dark Ale
Notes: Rich dark fruit character. Pairs perfectly with Special B and dark candi sugar.

### Wyeast 3787 (Trappist High Gravity)
Attenuation: 76% | Temp: 18–26°C
Flavor: Fruity, spicy, complex, warming
Best for: Belgian Tripel, Dubbel, Belgian Strong, Saison (secondary)
Notes: Handles high gravity well. Classic Trappist character. Ferment warm for complexity.

### Wyeast 3944 (Belgian Witbier)
Attenuation: 72% | Temp: 17–24°C
Flavor: Tart, fruity, spicy, phenolic
Best for: Witbier, Belgian White, Bière Blanche
Notes: Produces classic Witbier tartness and spice. Lower temps for more phenolic character.

### Wyeast 1318 / WLP066 (London Ale III)
Attenuation: 71–73% | Temp: 17–23°C
Flavor: Fruity, full-bodied, juicy
Best for: NEIPA / Hazy IPA (top choice), Hazy Pale, English Pale
Notes: **Critical for NEIPA** — produces biotransformation compounds that enhance hop fruitiness. Retains residual sweetness and full body. Do NOT use for West Coast IPA (too fruity, not dry).

## Specialty Yeasts

### Kveik (Lallemand Voss)
Attenuation: 80% | Temp: 25–40°C
Flavor: Orange, tropical at lower temps (25–30°C); neutral at high temp (38–40°C)
Best for: Any style wanting fast fermentation (24–48h), farmhouse ales
Notes: Extreme heat tolerance. Fermenting at 25–30°C produces orange/citrus esters.
At 38–40°C produces neutral, clean beer very quickly. No off-flavors at high temps.

## Attenuation & Body Notes

- **High attenuation (>75%)**: Dry finish, lower FG — use for IPA, Saison, Belgian Strong, any "dry" style
- **Low attenuation (<70%)**: Full body, residual sweetness — use for ESB, NEIPA, English stouts, malt-forward styles
- **FG target** = OG × (1 - attenuation %) — e.g., OG 1.060, 75% att → FG 1.015
- **Temperature effects**: Higher fermentation temp = more esters + fusel alcohols. Critical for Hefeweizen and Belgian styles.
